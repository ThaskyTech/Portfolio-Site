6-Axis Servo Arm with Vision

A DIY robotic arm combined with object detection and depth estimation, built and demonstrated in four days.

STATUSShipped (showcase project)
TIMELINE4 days
HARDWAREArduino Uno, servos (PWM)
SOFTWAREPython, YOLOv8, MiDaS

What it does

A six-axis arm driven by servos from an Arduino Uno, with a camera feeding a Python pipeline. YOLOv8 finds objects in the frame and MiDaS estimates depth from a single camera image, giving the arm a sense of where things are.

What got done

  • Camera calibration completed.
  • Object detection and monocular depth estimation running in Python.
  • Servo control over PWM from the Arduino.

Constraints and decisions

Time. With four days, I chose a reliable demo over a half-working one: the final demonstration runs a hardcoded motion sequence on the Arduino rather than closing the full vision-to-motion loop.

Power. The servos run from a 12V supply, so a buck converter steps it down to 5V to protect them.

Next steps

Close the loop: turn detections and depth into joint targets, and add inverse kinematics so the arm moves to what the camera sees.
